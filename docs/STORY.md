# Story

## Frame
The gods sit around a board on Olympus. The war below is their game; the heroes are their pieces. The player moves the Greek pieces. The gods comment, cheat and lose interest. Mortals never see the board; the player always does.

## Acts
- **Act I: The Beaches.** The Greeks land and hold the shore and the ships. (Iliad, early books: the landing is pre-Iliad tradition; the fight at the ships is Books 13–15.)
- **Act II: The Plain of Troy.** The Scamander, the chariots, Patroclus in borrowed armor, Apollo's plague, Hector at the Scaean Gates. (Books 1, 5, 16, 21–22.)
- **Act III: The Walls.** Achilles' wrath, Aphrodite's rescues, Paris on the walls, the wooden horse, the gates open. (Books 3, 20–22; then the Epic Cycle and Odyssey 8 for the horse and Achilles' death.)

## Scenes (expanded story, per the kickoff answers)
- Act intro: 3–4 vase panels, one line each. Skippable.
- Pre-level: 2–4 short lines (a mortal speaks, a god remarks). Post-level: 1–2 lines.
- Story beats: L8 Patroclus falls in the story after the level (whatever the player did on the board: the gods decided long ago); L10 Hector and the duel set-up; L13 Paris's fate shown tastefully as a vase scene.
- All text lives in `src/data/strings.ts`.

## Tone rules
Spare, mythic, a little wry about the gods. Short lines (≤50 characters per line, ≤2 lines per box). Concrete nouns. Gods are shown petty through actions. Stylized violence only; nothing gory.

**Banned phrases:** "embark on an epic journey", "unleash", "in a world where", "delve", "tapestry", "testament to", "elevate", "seamless", "immersive experience", "Are you ready?", exclamation-mark spam, em-dash-heavy prose, and any line a generic fantasy game could also use.

## Allowed sources for epigraphs (public domain only)
Samuel Butler (1898, prose); Lang, Leaf & Myers (1883, prose); Alexander Pope (1715–20, verse). One short line per screen at most; credit each in CREDITS.md. Never quote modern translations.

*Scene text is written in M5–M6 by the lead and `lore-keeper`.*
